# Theory and Coursework

Use this family when the deck is driven by concepts, arguments, close reading, social-science discussion, or classroom interpretation.

## Default Direction

`black-white-argument`

## Directions

### black-white-argument

- Best for: theory discussion, classroom presentation, philosophy and social-science argument
- What it looks like: black, white, and gray pages with direct thesis-led headings, bullet arguments, and concept contrast blocks
- Cover syntax: large title, low decoration, argument-first framing
- Section syntax: number plus thesis-like section sentence
- Content grammar: concept contrast, logical progression, quote boxes, direct conclusion bullets
- Preserve: clarity of argument, sobriety, typographic hierarchy
- Avoid: overly decorative imagery or unrelated visual metaphors

### paper-quotation

- Best for: close reading, classic text interpretation, lecture-style coursework, annotated discussion
- What it looks like: warm paper base, lecture-note or printed-page feeling, text-centered pages with quotation emphasis
- Cover syntax: printed-page or lecture-note mood
- Section syntax: handout-like or annotated reading-page divider
- Content grammar: long quotation, marginal note, paragraph breakdown, author comparison
- Preserve: reading rhythm, textual intimacy, editorial calm
- Avoid: using it for decks that are mostly charts or engineering diagrams

### cool-gray-concept

- Best for: tech ethics, social impact, interdisciplinary concept reports, abstract issue framing
- What it looks like: cool gray modern frames, relation maps, concept blocks, quiet but contemporary tone
- Cover syntax: quiet modern concept framing
- Section syntax: concept-led chapter title
- Content grammar: relation maps, issue decomposition, conclusion lists
- Preserve: abstraction, restraint, conceptual order
- Avoid: adding emotional poster energy when the task is analytical
