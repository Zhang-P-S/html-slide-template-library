# Policy and Governance

Use this family when the deck must read as institutional, public-facing, structured, or policy-led.

## Default Direction

`red-gold-outline`

## Directions

### red-gold-outline

- Best for: policy outline, plan interpretation, task briefing, chapter-based official reporting
- What it looks like: red and gold structure, formal title weight, strong chapter transitions, policy lists and key-number pages
- Cover syntax: centered or near-centered heavyweight title, ceremonial framing, stable public-briefing tone
- Section syntax: “Part X” plus title, visible red tags or capsules
- Content grammar: task checklist, key numbers, timeline, policy explanation blocks
- Preserve: authority, ceremony, structural clarity
- Avoid: turning it into a scholarship defense or startup pitch

### party-history-documentary

- Best for: themed education, party-history narrative, commemorative red-culture storytelling
- What it looks like: stronger atmosphere, more image-led than outline-led, with memory and narrative weight
- Cover syntax: ribbon, skyline, memorial architecture, narrative atmosphere
- Section syntax: title-led divider with image atmosphere
- Content grammar: event timeline, image documentary, spirit summary, retrospection
- Preserve: narrative weight, commemorative rhythm
- Avoid: file-like dense text blocks on every slide

### blue-red-briefing

- Best for: modern governance, industrial policy, digital strategy, technology-policy crossover
- What it looks like: more modern and restrained than red-gold, with navy structure and policy seriousness
- Cover syntax: modern sans-serif briefing look instead of ceremonial poster style
- Section syntax: horizontal briefing dividers
- Content grammar: framework diagrams, indicator summary, project examples, governance modules
- Preserve: public-briefing clarity with a more contemporary visual language
- Avoid: drifting into generic consulting or product deck aesthetics
